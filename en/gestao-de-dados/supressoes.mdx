Suppression is the list of who **never receives anything, under any circumstances** — applied automatically to every send, campaign or flow. It exists to protect two things: the rights of those who left, and your sender reputation.

## The four categories

In **Audience › Contacts › Suppressions**:

- **Unsubscribes** — people who clicked to leave (through the email link or Gmail/Yahoo one-click unsubscribe). They enter on their own, immediately.
- **Complaints** — people who marked your email as spam. They enter automatically via provider feedback. This is the most expensive signal: above 0.1% of sends, your reputation suffers.
- **Hard bounces** — addresses that don't exist. After the permanent rejection, the contact is suppressed so it doesn't generate new bounces.
- **Blocklist** — people **you** add: individual emails (with an optional reason) or in bulk.

Automatic entries appear as **protected** — reversing one requires contacting support, on purpose: re-subscribing someone who complained or bounced hurts your delivery.

## Import your previous ESP's suppression list

If you migrated from another tool, this is a **mandatory step before your first send**: export the suppression list there (bounces, complaints, unsubscribes) and import it here under **Bulk import** — paste the addresses or upload a CSV/TXT.

Without it, your first dispatch "rediscovers" one by one all the dead addresses your previous tool already knew about — a spike of bounces that providers read as a bad sender. The old tool paid for that learning; bring it with you.

Entire invalid domains can be blocked too — and dead-domain protection is shared: the base of invalid domains protecting your account improves with every customer on the platform.

## What suppression is not

- **It is not a campaign exclusion list** — to keep an audience out of one specific send, use "Exclude from audience" in the campaign.
- **It does not make your data disappear** — a suppressed contact stays in your base, with its history; it just doesn't receive anything.
