## What you will learn

What flows are, which come ready-made, how to activate your first one and how to track performance.

## What a flow is

A flow (sometimes called a drip sequence) works on its own: a **trigger** puts the contact in (a signup, an abandoned cart, a purchase), and the **steps** send messages by email and WhatsApp with the delays and conditions you define. Anyone who converts along the way **exits the flow** — no one gets "come back to your cart" after buying.

## The ready-made templates

Under **Automations › Flows**, the gallery brings the validated flows, ready to turn on in a few clicks and adjust later on the canvas:

- 👋 **Welcome** — three emails spread over the first days after signup, up to the first purchase. Whoever buys, exits — whoever hasn't bought yet keeps receiving the sequence.
- 🛒 **Abandoned cart** — fires shortly after the customer leaves your site with items in the cart (requires the pixel installed); touches by email and WhatsApp, with a coupon on the second contact.
- ⭐ **Post-purchase** — "did everything arrive okay?" with the review answered **inside the email** (requires the store integration or orders coming in).

Each card shows its state ("Use template" or "In use — view flow"). Templates marked **Coming soon** (browse abandonment, price drop, reorder) are not available yet.

## Activate your first one

1. Choose **Welcome** — it has the fewest prerequisites.
2. Review the three emails (each step uses an editor template — swap in your own if you like).
3. **Activate flow.** Activation validates everything; if any step is incomplete (for example, missing a template), the screen points to the exact block.

Who enters the welcome flow: contacts who **just signed up** with consent (popup, form inside the email, manual signup). **An imported base does not enter** — an import is not a new signup ([understand why](/en/automacoes/gatilhos)).

## Tracking: the live funnel

Every active flow shows its funnel in real time: how many entered, how many are **waiting now** at each step, how many received, opened, clicked — and, at the end, who **exited on conversion** versus who finished without converting. A paused flow shows its numbers frozen, and marked as such.

## Pausing is not deactivating

**Pausing freezes**: whoever is inside stays where they are and resumes from the same point when you reactivate. A flow that has already sent messages cannot be deleted — deactivate it; the report history stays intact.
