The pixel is a snippet you paste into your store. With it, Cliqo learns **what someone did before buying** — which product they looked at, which category they browsed, where they stopped — and builds segments from that on its own.

Without the pixel, Cliqo only knows who is already on your list and what they already bought. With it, you can reach people who showed interest and never came back.

## The snippet

In **Settings → Integrations**, the *Tracking Pixel* panel shows your snippet already personalised, with a **Copy all** button. It looks like this:

```html
<script async src="https://app.cliqo.io/px.js" data-tenant="YOUR-ID"></script>
```

Paste it in the `<head>` of **every page** of the store — not just the home page. It has to be the `<head>`, not the end of `<body>`: the pixel needs to load before the content to measure time on page correctly.

## Where to paste it, by platform

The panel offers three routes:

- **Loja Integrada** — a button opens that platform's panel on the right screen.
- **Google Tag Manager** — a button opens GTM.
- **Manual** — for any other store: paste it into your theme template.

**If you use GTM, use the snippet the panel gives you for GTM.** Tag Manager strips `data-*` attributes, and `data-tenant` is one of them — the pixel would load without knowing whose store it is. The GTM version carries your identifier inside the script address (`?t=…`), which GTM cannot strip.

## Confirming it works

The same panel has a **Verify now** button. After pasting the snippet, open a page of your store in another tab and click it.

⚠️ **Read the result carefully — it answers a narrower question than it appears to.** The check counts hits from the **last 24 hours**. It does not answer "is the pixel installed?", it answers "did any traffic arrive in the last 24 hours?".

In practice:

- **Pixel active** — traffic arrived. You are set.
- **Awaiting installation** — no traffic in the last 24 hours. That can mean the pixel is not installed **or** that it is installed on a low-traffic store having a quiet day.

If your store has low traffic and the screen says "awaiting installation", open one of your own store pages and click **Verify now** again. If the hit shows up, it is installed.

## What it captures on its own

You do not have to tag anything. Once installed, the pixel identifies the page type and emits the events below — and each one feeds an automatic segment under **Contacts → Segments**:

| Event | Feeds the segment |
|---|---|
| Page view | All visitors |
| Product view | Browsed without buying |
| Category browse | Category interest |
| Add to cart | Almost bought |
| Checkout start | Checkout abandonment |

Beyond those, the pixel measures **scroll depth** on product pages (25%, 50%, 75%, 90%) and **real time on page** — interest signals that open rate cannot capture.

To recognise the page type it first reads your theme's standard markup (`og:type`, `og:title`, `og:price`), then structured product data, and only then falls back to heuristics. That is what makes it work on any platform, with no dedicated integration.

## Recording a purchase

When the customer completes the order, call this on the thank-you page:

```js
window.cliqo.purchase(value, orderId, productName);
```

This is what closes the loop: without this call, Cliqo sees the browsing but never learns it became a sale, and attributed revenue comes out understated.

If the person has identified themselves on the store (login, or email at checkout), you can also tie the anonymous browsing to the contact:

```js
window.cliqo.identify(contactId);
```

## Privacy and LGPD

The pixel stores an anonymous identifier in the visitor's browser and records which pages they saw. Under Brazil's LGPD that is **pseudonymised personal data**, even with no name or email attached.

Two obligations that are yours, not Cliqo's:

1. **Your privacy policy must state that the store uses a tracking pixel**, what it is for, and who the data is shared with.
2. **If your store shows a cookie notice, the pixel belongs in it.**

None of this blocks using it — it is the same framing as any analytics tool. It just has to be written down.

## When to stop waiting and ask for help

If the snippet is in the `<head>`, you visited the store, and the panel still records nothing, the most common cause is the snippet sitting in a template that page does not use. Check by viewing the page source in your browser and searching for `px.js`. If it is not there, it never loaded.

If it persists, write to us at [contato@cliqo.io](mailto:contato@cliqo.io) with the address of a page where it should be.
